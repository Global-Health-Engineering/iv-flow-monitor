# Bench Run V_50_04 — 2026-05-13

Fourth run of the 2026-05-13 campaign, intended as the first validation of the **mean-pulse firmware patch** (`tau_s` derived from `(pulse_top + pulse_bot) / 2` rather than `pulse_top` alone). Treat the numbers below as **exploratory** — the UART stream contains a 248-second mid-run gap that the scale captured through, so V_50_04 is not on equal footing with V_50_01..03.

## Anomalies / operator notes

- **248-second UART gap between drop_N 66 (abs_ms 435017) and drop_N 67 (abs_ms 683756).** No DROP rows emitted during this window. The scale continued streaming through the gap. Cause not fully characterised:
  - Drip rate at the bench fluctuated during the run (15 inter-drop intervals exceeded 2× the median 4.28 s).
  - Some of the gap is plausibly real dripping pause; some is plausibly drops the device failed to register (signal swing transiently below `CAL_MARGIN_HIGH=100`).
  - The two effects cannot be disentangled from this run alone — they require an instrumented "phantom" drop generator.
- A `DROP_REJECT,reason=vol_hi` fired at abs_ms 693620 (between drops 68 and 69) with `V_uL_tenths=2147483647` (int32 overflow), indicating a malformed pulse pair. A second reject at abs_ms 735149 had `V_uL_tenths=3233117` (323 µL). Both clipped at the firmware's `VOL_UL_MAX` guard rather than polluting the volume-step trend.
- Drops 44..52 then 67..83 form two relatively clean stretches separated by the gap.

## Gravimetric

The scale stream gives a clean net mass over the 600-second window:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Mass at scale t=0 | 14.5205 g (residual from prior runs, not tared) |
| Mass at scale t=600 s | 21.9646 g |
| Net mass | **7.4441 g** |
| Volume (ρ=1.000) | 7.4441 mL |
| Gravimetric flow rate over 600 s | **44.66 mL/hr** |
| Per-UART-drop V_true_chamber (assuming 40 drops are correct) | **186.10 µL/drop** |

The per-drop value is unusually high because the UART count likely under-represents the actual chamber-drop count over the same window (see anomaly note above). It is reported here as a **lower bound** on N_chamber and therefore an **upper bound** on V_true_chamber.

## Per-drop measurement summary

Reading the firmware-output V from the 40 surviving DROP rows (firmware now applies `V_CAL_K=1.27`):

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| N drops in CSV | 40 |
| V (firmware, calibrated) — median | 152.4 µL |
| V (firmware, calibrated) — range | 142.1 – 173.9 µL |
| pulse_TOP — median | 4680 µs |
| pulse_BOT — median | 8163 µs |
| BOT/TOP pulse ratio | 1.75× (vs physics-expected 0.92×) |
| transit_us — median | 9540 µs |
| v_TOP — median | ≈ 1.03 m/s |

Drops in V_50_04 are notably larger (≈150 µL firmware-calibrated, ≈186 µL gravimetric) than V_50_01..03 (40-74 µL gravimetric). This appears to be a genuine consequence of the slow clamp setting — at low flow rate, fluid accumulates at the chamber tip longer before detaching, producing larger drops.

## Why this run is preserved despite the gap

V_50_04 is the only run in the 2026-05-13 campaign at the slow end of the operating envelope (≈45 mL/hr gravimetric). Together with V_streaming at the fast end (269 mL/hr) and V_50_01..03 in the middle (64–93 mL/hr), it bounds the device's tested operating range. The 40 surviving drops also confirm that the firmware mean-pulse patch produces V estimates much closer to the gravimetric per-drop average than the pre-patch TOP-only firmware did (k ≈ 1.13 vs ≈ 6 with TOP-only at this drop size).
