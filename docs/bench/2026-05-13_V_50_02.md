## Per-drop validation summary

Via `tools/build_bench_overlay.py` on the V_50_02 pair (gravity-corrected mean-of-pulses model, identical physics to the firmware) with `V_CAL_K = 1.27` applied:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| N drops | 339 |
| Mean V_est (uncalibrated, gravity-corrected) | 39.24 µL |
| Mean V_est (calibrated, × V_CAL_K) | 49.84 µL |
| `k_pre = V_true / V_est_uncal` | **1.001** |
| `k_post = V_true / V_est_cal` | **0.788** (device over-reads by 21 %) |
| Per-drop CV (uncalibrated) | 5.5 % |

V_50_02 has the **smallest drops in the calibration set** (39.26 µL true) and is the only run where the uncalibrated chord-sphere model matches truth almost perfectly (k_pre ≈ 1.00). After applying the global `V_CAL_K = 1.27` (which is tuned to handle the average across all four runs), V_50_02 is over-corrected — k_post drops to 0.79. This is the worst-case under-correction direction in the calibration set and pins one edge of the ±30 % residual quoted in `docs/results.md`.

## Why does the uncalibrated model match V_50_02 best?

V_50_02 ran at the **highest in-envelope flow rate** (83.55 mL/h, period 1.69 s) with the **smallest drops** of the four campaigns. Two compounding effects make these conditions favourable for the chord-sphere model:

1. **Smaller drops are more spherical.** Surface tension dominates over inertia for small drops, suppressing the oscillation between oblate and prolate that biases the chord-time → diameter conversion on larger drops. The chord measured at the beam plane is closer to the equivalent-sphere diameter.
2. **TOP/BOT asymmetry partially cancels in the mean-of-pulses.** V_50_02 had the highest BOT/TOP ratio of the campaign (2.50×), but with TOP pulse small (3190 µs) and BOT pulse roughly the same as other runs (8013 µs), the mean ends up close to the geometric chord.

Both effects break down as drops get larger (V_50_01, V_50_04), where the residual is in the device-under-reads direction. The single-scalar `V_CAL_K` cannot capture this drop-size dependence; the residual is captured as drop-shape oscillation in `docs/limitations.md` §1.
